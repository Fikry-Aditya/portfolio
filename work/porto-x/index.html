Scaling E-commerce Growth Through Paid Media & Conversion

A performance marketing case study covering audience development, creative direction, budget scaling, marketplace growth, and conversion measurement across Porto X.

Business Context

Building a clearer performance system for a growing e-commerce brand.

Porto X is a recovery footwear brand selling through its own website and marketplace channels, with paid acquisition supporting both direct response and long-term e-commerce growth.

When I joined in October 2025, Shopee performance was already relatively stable. However, the broader paid media setup still lacked clear audience direction, consistent creative logic, and enough structured data to confidently determine what should be scaled.

My role was to create a more structured approach across audience testing, creative direction, budget allocation, measurement, and conversion.

Starting Point

The account was active. The learning system was not clear yet.

The first challenge was not simply increasing spend. It was creating cleaner signals from audience, creative, and conversion performance before making larger scaling decisions.

01

Unclear audience direction

Audience targeting existed, but segmentation and learning were not structured enough to clearly understand which user groups were producing stronger purchase signals.

02

Fragmented creative direction

Creative messaging needed a clearer role across evergreen product communication, campaign moments, and lower-funnel conversion messaging.

03

Not enough usable data

Before scaling aggressively, I needed enough purchase data to understand which audiences, products, and campaign structures could absorb more budget efficiently.

Growth Timeline

From account cleanup to validated scale.

The growth did not happen from one campaign change. It developed as audience signals, creative direction, and scaling confidence became stronger over time.

Oct 2025

Establish the baseline

Joined Porto X and reviewed the existing setup. Shopee was relatively stable, while paid media still needed clearer audience and creative direction before reliable scaling decisions could be made.

Nov 2025

Rebuild the testing structure

Audience testing was reorganised around interest groups, purchase-based lookalikes, and available retargeting signals. Creative direction and budget logic were also tied more closely to purchase performance and ROAS.

Dec 2025

Identify stronger purchase signals

Purchase-based lookalikes began to show stronger potential within CPAS. Interest audiences remained useful for testing and expansion, while higher-intent segments helped clarify where future budget could be concentrated.

Jan 2026

Hold before scaling

Retargeting remained an important contributor while lookalike budgets were still controlled. The objective was to validate stability before increasing spend and risking efficiency.

Feb 2026

Scale validated audiences

Purchase-based lookalikes started becoming the dominant growth audience. Budget was increased more confidently while ROAS and purchase efficiency remained the primary guardrails.

Mar 2026

Multiple growth levers converged

Audience maturity, stronger SKU focus, creative refreshes, seasonal demand, campaign pricing, and disciplined scaling came together during the strongest period of growth.

Budget & Scaling Logic

Scale was earned through purchase efficiency.

Budget was not increased simply because CTR or engagement looked good. The primary decision signals remained purchases and ROAS.

01 · Test

Create cleaner signals

Use structured audience and creative testing to establish reliable purchase behaviour.

02 · Validate

Check purchase efficiency

Evaluate ROAS, purchase volume, and campaign stability before increasing spend.

03 · Scale

Increase selectively

Prioritise campaigns and audiences that continued meeting commercial targets as spend increased.

04 · Review

Optimise weekly

Reassess budget allocation based on current purchase and revenue performance rather than historical winners alone.

Creative Strategy

Brand story, product winner, then conversion pressure.

Paid creative was designed around Porto X's existing product narrative while adapting the message to purchase intent and campaign timing.

Brand USP

Evergreen communication focused on recovery, arch support, and Porto X's AXtiveRest technology to stay consistent with the brand's organic product story.

Product Winner

Best-selling SKUs and categories such as clogs and slides were prioritised through Commerce Manager product organisation and product-focused creative.

Conversion Urgency

Lower-funnel messaging used campaign pricing, checkout urgency, and stock pressure to convert users who were already closer to purchase.

Key Learnings

What the scaling period reinforced.

01

Scaling is not just increasing budget.

Spend should follow validated purchase efficiency. Budget growth without strong conversion signals can increase volume while weakening commercial efficiency.

02

Creative and audience need to learn together.

Strong targeting cannot compensate for unclear product messaging. Better performance came from combining audience maturity with consistent product and conversion communication.

03

Backend outcomes matter more than platform vanity.

CTR and engagement are useful signals, but purchase, CPA, ROAS, and backend conversion remained the final criteria for scaling decisions.
